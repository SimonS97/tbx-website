# Market research: TTRPG publisher homepage product discovery

**Decision this research serves:** Choose between one featured product or up to three curated product entry paths for Tales by Xero.

## Executive Summary

**Recommendation: use up to three curated product entry paths, each organized around a visitor need rather than a generic product grid.** This fits a catalog that contains different product kinds and has no single permanent flagship. It also matches the user's stated discovery questions: a quick adventure, a flexible campaign framework, or a smaller item-oriented product.

The evidence does not establish a conversion-rate winner between one and three features. It does establish that official TTRPG publisher sites commonly offer more than one discovery path and make the paths legible through product purpose, play context, or product category rather than presenting an undifferentiated catalog. MCDM promotes distinct offer types, Free League routes visitors through genre and play context, and Mothership separates core entry, collection options, and a module library.[1][2][3]

For Tales by Xero, the homepage should not use a giant atmospheric hero followed by a traditional product gallery. Instead, its first viewport should contain a compact brand cue plus a visible **"Find your next session"** trio. Each path leads to a different real product and has one sentence that names the visitor's need. The Saffron Myth Theatre language should make the trio feel like three opening scenes, not three ecommerce cards.

## Decision Frame

### Hard constraints

- No permanent single product currently deserves to dominate the brand.
- The site is a portfolio and discovery surface, not a storefront; final handoff goes to DriveThruRPG.
- The homepage must expose real content and an actionable next step above the fold.
- The visual system remains Saffron Myth Theatre, with the Field Guide's genre-open discovery principle.

### Weighted preferences

- Help visitors self-identify the kind of material they need quickly.
- Represent the breadth of a solo catalog without manufacturing empty density.
- Preserve a high-art-direction presentation rather than a conventional shop grid.
- Keep the structure reversible as the product catalog changes.

## Official Publisher Pattern Scan

### MCDM: Multiple offers framed by use

MCDM's public home page presents several distinct products and product classes: a new RPG, a starter adventure, an epic campaign, and supplements. The page gives each offer a clear use-oriented sentence, such as "Everything you need to play ... tonight" for The Delian Tomb and "Epic Campaign" for Crack the Sun, then sends the visitor to a purchase or follow destination.[1]

**Transferable lesson:** Multiple offers can coexist when each answers a different intent. **Not transferable:** MCDM's scale, owned store, and large back catalog.

### Free League: Route through intent before product depth

Free League routes visitors through genre (Fantasy, Horror, Sci-Fi), play context (At the Table, Online, Solo), and a getting-started route before requiring a specific product choice.[2]

**Transferable lesson:** Visitors can be invited to choose the kind of experience they want rather than being asked to understand the catalog first. **Not transferable:** Tales by Xero is Daggerheart-focused, so genre-level routing would be too broad as a primary taxonomy.

### Mothership: Separate the initial entry from the wider library

Mothership introduces the core game proposition, distinguishes Core and Deluxe routes, and later frames modules as a broader library of campaigns, rules supplements, and in-universe texts.[3]

**Transferable lesson:** An entry page can make one clear initial choice while still acknowledging varied content types later. **Not transferable:** Tales by Xero should not imitate a boxed-product comparison or a storefront hierarchy.

## Recommended Tales by Xero Structure

### First viewport

Replace the rejected large empty hero with a compact, asymmetric scene header. Keep the Harmony-style image fade as a mood carrier, but reserve at least half of the visible content area for a three-route product selector.

The selector should be visible above the fold on desktop and begin within the first viewport on mobile. It contains no more than three options:

1. **Run something tonight** — a real One-Shot product. Supporting line: one playable premise and one tangible table promise.
2. **Build a campaign around it** — a real Campaign Framework. Supporting line: what flexibility or story engine it gives a GM.
3. **Bring something to the table** — a real printable item, cheat-sheet-like resource, or other small-format product. If no suitable released item exists at launch, omit this route rather than inventing one.

### Interaction model

- On pointer hover or keyboard focus, a route brings forward its image crop, saffron stage line, short hook, and clear external destination.
- On touch, the route opens its real product detail page; do not require hover to reveal essential information.
- Only one route is visually in the foreground at a time. The other two remain readable and immediately selectable.
- The selector is a visual "fan" or layered stage arrangement, not a carousel, auto-rotating deck, or equal-size card grid.

### Why this fits the product

This makes the homepage answer the user's actual question in the user's own words: "What kind of session do I need?" It does not pretend that one product represents all of Tales by Xero, and it lets different tones coexist under a stable interaction pattern.

## When One Featured Product Wins Instead

Use one featured product temporarily when a release has an explicit launch window, campaign, award, or substantial new asset set that makes it the clear current story. The rest of the three-route selector can remain immediately below it. This is a content rotation choice, not a different homepage architecture.

## Risks and Guards

- **Risk:** Three routes recreate a generic card grid. **Guard:** use unequal stage positions, shared visual choreography, and intent-first labels; limit to three.
- **Risk:** Product types become abstract or overlap. **Guard:** each route must point to a real released work and use concrete visitor language, not internal taxonomy alone.
- **Risk:** A thin catalog makes the page look padded. **Guard:** show only released routes; collapse to two if only two strong products exist.
- **Risk:** Motion delays discovery. **Guard:** all route labels, hooks, and links render immediately; animation only changes emphasis.

## Recommendation

Adopt a **three-route "Find your next session" opener** as the default homepage entry. It should replace the failed empty hero, not be placed after it. Begin with current representatives such as a One-Shot, Abythera as a Campaign Framework, and one released item-oriented work when available. Keep a single product spotlight as a replaceable campaign layer for future launches, not as the permanent homepage structure.
