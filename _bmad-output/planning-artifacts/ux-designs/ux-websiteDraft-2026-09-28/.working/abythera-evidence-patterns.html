Abythera / evidence pattern study

How should product proof live on the page?

Each direction uses the same four Abythera assets. The question is only how a visitor finds, scans, and opens them when a product has several real things worth showing.

Direction 01Evidence ReaderOne selected proof, a clear index, and a full inspection route. Direction 02StorylineEach image has a full editorial beat in a deliberate sequence. Direction 03Proof IndexSee everything quickly, then open the item that earns attention.

Direction 01 / Recommended

One usable proof, always in focus.

The page keeps moving vertically, but the supporting material does not become four or five repeated screen-length chapters. Choose a proof, inspect it, then continue reading.

A complete, uncropped page rather than a decorative screenshot.

Best when product evidence grows

Four or five assets remain a controlled part of the detail page. The visitor sees an index, a useful selected proof, and a single full-size inspection route without being sent through an endless wall of images.

Direction 02 / Editorial sequence

Let each piece change the visitor's understanding.

This is the high-impact route: every asset earns a dedicated narrative beat. It is cinematic and clear, but it deliberately makes the detail page longer as proof grows.

Best when the proof itself is the story

This makes a small, carefully chosen set of assets feel authored and premium. With four or five assets it is substantially longer, so it works best when every image genuinely changes the buying decision.

Direction 03 / Compact proof index

See the whole evidence set before choosing a page.

This is the shortest route through the material. The grid makes breadth immediately visible, while a full-size dialog handles the detail that a smaller document preview cannot carry.

Best when page length is the priority

It is fast to scan and easy to extend. The tradeoff is intentional: document pages become only recognisable at a glance, so the dialog must do real work for visitors who need to judge the details.
